## **Transaction in Bitcoin system**
Let's take a look at a sample transaction in a Bitcoin system. The scenario we'll consider is where Ross wants to send 5 bitcoins to Joey. You can see below how this transaction happens. 
Step 1, Joey shares his address with Ross. 
Step 2 Ross creates a transaction message and adds Joey's address and five Bitcoins to it. 
Step 3 Ross then signs the transaction with this private key and shares his public key to the network for signature verification.
Step 4, Ross broadcast the transaction over the Bitcoin network. Thus, Ross has successfully sent a transaction of five bitcoins to Joey.

## Double spending problem
Now let's see what happens when Joey tries to spend the same B. T. C. In more than one transaction, which is potentially a double spending case, which is a common scenario in Bitcoin blockchain. The example that we will consider is where Joey, who has five bitcoins in his wallet, is trying to spend them on more than 1 transaction. 
As you can see, Joey is trying to send five bitcoins to Rachel and 5 bitcoins to Monica. This act of spending the same Bitcoin in more than one transaction is called the double spending problem. 

**Encountering double spending in blockchain** 
However, blockchain overcomes this double spending problem. The security measures which prevent double spending. The security measures which prevent double spending and blockchain are as follows. 
- Transaction details are sent to all nodes which are on the network. This is the process of initiation and the broadcast of transaction. 
- Blockchain is constantly growing in each pair maintains their own copy of blockchain. This is defined as the distributed Ledger 
- blockchain ensures that any modification in the block will lead to recomputing of the following blocks until a consensus is reached.
-  Anyone can validate the transactions and this validation prevents the issue of double depending.

## Bitcoin transaction structure
Now let's take a look at a Bitcoin transaction structure. A Bitcoin transaction has three pieces of information. 1st piece of information is called an input. This is a record of which Bitcoin address was used to send the Bitcoins to the receiver in the first place. It is all defined as the unspent transaction output. These bitcoins have been received by the user in the past and have not been spent, thus the term unspent transaction output.
The second piece of information is the amount. This is the amount of bitcoins that the sender sends to the receiver. This is a new transaction  that the sender is initiating where he uses the receiver's public key and his own private key to hash the transaction and send the same.
the last piece of information is the output. This is sent to the sender's address as change the bank balance amount is sent back to the receiver and it becomes the new unspent transaction output for his wallet.
